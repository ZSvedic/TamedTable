# Taglines

The line that goes under the wordmark and at the top of the site. It owns the short hook; the reasoning behind it lives in [positioning.md](positioning.md).

Every tagline here is the one-liner from [positioning.md](positioning.md) compressed to a few words — say what you want, get a recipe you keep. Pick one primary; the alternates fit different surfaces.

## Primary

> **Talk to your data. Keep the recipe.**

Two beats: the ease (talk) and the payoff that sets us apart (a replayable spec you own). Works in a navbar, a tweet, or a conference badge.

## Alternates

By surface and emphasis:

| Tagline | Lean | Use for |
|---|---|---|
| **ETL you can just ask for.** | Ease, dev-friendly | Hacker News, dev landing pages |
| **Clean tables by saying so.** | Plainest, non-technical | Ops/finance audiences |
| **Spreadsheets that take instructions.** | Familiar metaphor | First-time visitors |
| **Describe it once, replay it forever.** | The flat-cost / reuse hook | Engineers comparing to scripts |
| **Your data, your words, your recipe.** | Ownership / open-source | About page, footer |

## Don't

- Don't claim "no code" without the second half — the recipe *is* code, just code you didn't write and don't maintain. "No code to write" is honest; "no code" is not.
- Don't say "AI spreadsheet" — it's an ETL engine with a table view, and the flat-cost spec is the story, not the AI.
- Don't promise magic ("perfect data in one click"). The honest pitch — you ask, you watch, you keep the recipe — is stronger than hype.
